## Overview

After facial contouring, swelling is considerable and usually peaks in the first 3–5 days. Final contours are often seen at 6–12 months. Always follow the specific instructions from your own clinic, as recommendations vary by individual.

## Procedure day

Rest with the head raised and wear the compression garment as instructed. Follow the liquid diet and take prescribed medicines.

## Days 1–3

Swelling and difficulty opening the mouth are common. Keep up careful oral hygiene, such as rinsing as instructed after meals.

## Days 4–7

A soft or liquid diet is commonly required for several weeks. Keep wearing compression as advised.

## Weeks 2–4

Many people need 2–3 weeks before feeling comfortable in public. Avoid strenuous exercise, alcohol and smoking for at least 4–6 weeks.

## After 1 month

Numbness of the lips, chin or cheeks usually improves gradually. Keep follow-up appointments, including imaging if your clinic requests it.

## Things to avoid

- Hard or chewy foods until your clinic allows them
- Alcohol and smoking for at least 4–6 weeks
- Strenuous exercise and contact sports

## When to contact the clinic

Contact your clinic if you notice:
- Swelling that suddenly increases on one side
- Numbness that is getting worse or a bad taste or smell in the mouth
- Swelling, bruising or pain that is getting worse after the first few days instead of better
- Redness, warmth or discharge at a treated or incision site
- Asymmetry or results you would like checked, especially before you leave Korea

## Warning signs — seek urgent care

Seek urgent medical attention (in Korea, call 119; verify current emergency numbers) and inform your clinic if you notice:
- Heavy bleeding that does not stop with gentle pressure, or rapidly increasing swelling on one side
- Fever, chills, or a wound that becomes hot, red and discharges pus
- Difficulty breathing, chest pain, or painful swelling in one leg (especially after flights)
