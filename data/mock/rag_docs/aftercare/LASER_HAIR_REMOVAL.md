## Overview

Laser hair removal usually has minimal downtime. It is done as a course of sessions, and treated hairs shed over the following weeks. Always follow the specific instructions from your own clinic, as recommendations vary by individual.

## Procedure day

Redness and small bumps around follicles are common. A cool compress may soothe the skin. Avoid hot baths, saunas and intense exercise for a day or two.

## Days 1–3

Redness typically settles within a day or two. Avoid sun exposure on treated areas and use sunscreen on exposed skin.

## Days 4–7

Treated hairs may shed over 1–3 weeks. Shaving is usually fine; do not pluck or wax.

## Weeks 2–4

Keep avoiding plucking and waxing between sessions. Clinics commonly schedule the next session several weeks later depending on the area.

## After 1 month

Results vary by hair and skin type and usually need several sessions.

## Things to avoid

- Plucking, waxing or epilating between sessions
- Sun exposure and tanning on treated areas
- Hot baths, saunas and intense exercise for a day or two

## When to contact the clinic

Contact your clinic if you notice:
- Blisters, crusting or darkening of the skin
- Redness or itching that lasts more than a few days
- Swelling, bruising or pain that is getting worse after the first few days instead of better
- Redness, warmth or discharge at a treated or incision site
- Asymmetry or results you would like checked, especially before you leave Korea

## Warning signs — seek urgent care

Seek urgent medical attention (in Korea, call 119; verify current emergency numbers) and inform your clinic if you notice:
- Blisters, open wounds or burns that are spreading or very painful
- Signs of infection such as increasing redness, warmth, pus or fever
- Signs of a severe allergic reaction such as facial or throat swelling
