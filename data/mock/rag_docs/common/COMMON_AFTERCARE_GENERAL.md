## Overview

This guide covers general aftercare that applies to many skin treatments and cosmetic surgeries. Always follow the specific instructions from your own clinic, as recommendations vary by procedure and by individual.

## Swelling and ice

- Swelling is normal after most procedures and often peaks within 48–72 hours after surgery.
- Cold compresses may help in the first 48 hours. Wrap ice packs in a cloth and apply in short intervals (for example, 10–20 minutes at a time) to avoid cold injury.
- After the first few days, some clinics suggest switching to warm compresses; ask before doing so.
- Avoid pressing or massaging treated areas unless your doctor instructs you to.

## Sleeping elevated

- For facial procedures, sleeping with the head raised on extra pillows for about 1–2 weeks may reduce swelling.
- Avoid sleeping face-down or on the treated side where advised.

## Sun protection

- Use a broad-spectrum sunscreen daily, wear a hat, and avoid strong sun, especially after lasers and skin treatments.
- Sun exposure on healing skin or scars may increase the risk of pigmentation.

## Things to avoid

- **Alcohol:** commonly avoided for at least a few days after injectables and several weeks after surgery, as it may increase swelling and bleeding.
- **Smoking:** may delay healing; avoid as long as advised.
- **Saunas, hot baths and jjimjilbang:** heat may worsen swelling; typically avoided for 1–2 weeks or longer after surgery.
- **Exercise:** light walking is often encouraged after surgery, but strenuous exercise is usually avoided for 1–6 weeks depending on the procedure.
- **Makeup and active skincare:** follow your clinic's timing, especially near incisions.

## Medications and wound care

- Take prescribed medicines as directed and ask before using additional painkillers, as some may increase bleeding.
- Keep incisions clean and dry as instructed, and do not remove stitches or dressings yourself.

## When to contact the clinic

Contact your clinic if you notice:
- Swelling or pain that is getting worse instead of better
- Increasing redness, warmth or discharge from a wound
- Fever
- Uneven results or concerns you would like checked before you leave Korea

## Warning signs — seek urgent care

Seek urgent medical attention (in Korea, call 119; verify current emergency numbers) if you notice:
- Sudden severe pain, skin turning pale, grey, blue or mottled after filler injections
- Any change in vision
- Difficulty breathing, chest pain or coughing up blood
- Painful swelling in one leg, especially after surgery or flights
- Heavy bleeding that does not stop with pressure
- Signs of severe allergic reaction such as facial or throat swelling

Bring your medication list and procedure details with you, and inform your clinic as soon as possible.
