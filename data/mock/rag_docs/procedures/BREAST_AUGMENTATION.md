## Overview

Breast augmentation increases breast size or improves shape and symmetry, most commonly with implants and sometimes with fat transfer. It is a major surgery with long-term considerations: implants are not lifetime devices and may need replacement or revision in the future, so ongoing follow-up after returning home is important.

**Also known as:** augmentation mammoplasty, breast implants, boob job, 가슴성형, 가슴확대, 유방확대술, 隆胸, 假体隆胸, 豊胸手術.

## How it works

Implants, typically silicone gel or saline shells, are placed either beneath the breast tissue or partly beneath the chest muscle. Incisions may be made in the underarm, the fold beneath the breast, or around the areola, each with trade-offs in scar visibility and control. Implant shape (round or anatomical), surface texture, size and profile are chosen based on body measurements and goals. Fat transfer offers a more modest increase using your own fat, with some reabsorption.

## Procedure steps

1. **Consultation:** chest measurements, implant sizing, imaging such as ultrasound, and health screening.
2. **Anaesthesia:** usually general anaesthesia.
3. **Surgery:** an incision is made, a pocket is created, and the implant is placed, often with endoscopic assistance for underarm approaches.
4. **Closure:** incisions are closed; drains may be used.
5. **Recovery:** observation, often overnight, and a supportive garment is applied.

Surgery typically takes about 1–2 hours.

## Recovery & downtime

Chest tightness, swelling and soreness are common, especially with under-muscle placement. Many people return to light activities and desk work after about 1 week, while heavy lifting and strenuous exercise are typically avoided for 4–6 weeks. Stitches or drains are commonly removed within about 1 week. Implants usually settle into a more natural position over 2–3 months. Clinics may recommend a support bra and specific massage or no massage, depending on the implant.

## Common side effects

- Pain, swelling, bruising and tightness
- Temporary or lasting changes in nipple or breast sensation
- Capsular contracture (hardening around the implant)
- Implant rupture, rippling, malposition or asymmetry, which may require revision
- Rarely, infection, haematoma or wound problems
- Rarely, breast implant-associated anaplastic large cell lymphoma (BIA-ALCL), mainly linked to some textured implants, and systemic symptoms some patients attribute to implants
- Rare but serious anaesthesia-related complications and blood clots

Discuss anaesthesia, rare serious complications and long-term implant care with your surgeon.

## Before your visit

- Bring any recent breast imaging and disclose family history of breast cancer.
- Stop smoking and review blood thinners and hormonal medications as advised.
- Plan at least 10–14 days in Korea and arrange a companion.
- Discuss flight timing with your surgeon, including clot prevention on long flights.
- Ask for implant identification details (manufacturer, model, size, lot number) for your records.

## Questions to ask your doctor

- Which implant type, size and placement do you recommend for my body, and why?
- What incision site will you use, and where will the scar be?
- Is the implant textured or smooth, and what are the long-term risks?
- What is your policy if I need revision for contracture or malposition?
- When can I fly home, and how should I arrange long-term follow-up and imaging?
