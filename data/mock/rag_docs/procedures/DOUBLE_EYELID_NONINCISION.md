## Overview

Non-incision double eyelid surgery creates an upper eyelid crease using fine sutures placed through tiny punctures, without a full-length skin incision. It is generally suited to people with thin eyelid skin, little excess skin or fat, and good eyelid-opening strength. Compared with the incision method it usually has shorter recovery and is more reversible, but the crease may loosen or fade over time.

**Also known as:** suture method, buried suture double eyelid, non-incisional blepharoplasty, 매몰법, 쌍꺼풀 매몰, 埋线双眼皮, 埋线法, 埋没法, 二重埋没.

## How it works

The surgeon marks the desired crease line, then passes fine sutures through small openings to link the eyelid skin to deeper structures (the levator muscle or tarsal plate). As the eye opens, the skin folds along this line, creating a crease. Some variations make very small openings to remove a little fat. The crease is maintained by the sutures and the scar tissue that forms around them. Longevity varies by individual; your doctor will advise.

## Procedure steps

1. **Consultation:** eyelid assessment, crease height simulation, and discussion of shape (for example, in-fold or out-fold).
2. **Anaesthesia:** usually local anaesthesia, sometimes with light sedation.
3. **Suturing:** sutures are placed through small punctures and tied and buried under the skin.
4. **Check and rest:** crease symmetry is checked, then a short recovery period at the clinic.

The procedure typically takes about 20–40 minutes.

## Recovery & downtime

Swelling and mild bruising are common and are usually most noticeable for 3–7 days. Many people feel comfortable in public after about 1 week, though the crease often looks higher or more defined at first and settles over 1–3 months. Clinics commonly advise cold compresses for the first 48 hours, sleeping with the head elevated, avoiding eye makeup and contact lenses for about 1 week, and avoiding alcohol, saunas and strenuous exercise for 1–2 weeks. A follow-up check within a few days is common.

## Common side effects

- Swelling, bruising and tightness
- Temporary asymmetry while swelling settles
- Crease loosening or disappearing over months to years, which may require redoing
- Suture-related issues such as a small bump, cyst or irritation of the eye surface
- Rarely, infection or prolonged dryness

As with any procedure, discuss the anaesthesia used and rare complications with your surgeon.

## Before your visit

- Stop wearing contact lenses as advised; bring glasses.
- Tell the clinic about dry eye, previous eyelid surgery and blood thinners.
- Plan at least 5–7 days in Korea, ideally including a follow-up visit.
- Discuss flight timing with your surgeon; mild swelling during air travel is common but should be cleared by them.

## Questions to ask your doctor

- Am I a good candidate for non-incision, or would the incision method last better for me?
- What crease height and shape suits my eyes?
- How many sutures and what technique will you use?
- What happens if the crease loosens, and is a revision covered?
- When can I fly home and wear makeup or contacts?
