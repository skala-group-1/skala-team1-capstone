## Overview

Facial contouring surgery reshapes the facial bones to change the outline of the face. Common procedures include mandible (square jaw) reduction, zygoma (cheekbone) reduction, and genioplasty (chin surgery), sometimes combined as a "V-line" surgery. It is a major operation and differs from injectable slimming, which only affects muscle or soft tissue.

**Also known as:** bone contouring, V-line surgery, jaw reduction, cheekbone reduction, 안면윤곽, 사각턱 수술, 광대축소, 턱끝 성형, 面部轮廓手术, 下颌角截骨, 颧骨内推, 輪郭形成, エラ削り.

## How it works

Most contouring is done through incisions inside the mouth, so external scars are usually avoided. In jaw reduction, part of the angle and outer layer of the mandible may be removed. In cheekbone reduction, the zygoma is cut and repositioned inward, and fixed with small plates or screws. In genioplasty, the chin bone is cut and moved forward, back or narrowed. Imaging such as CT scans is typically used to plan safe cuts away from nerves.

## Procedure steps

1. **Consultation and imaging:** facial analysis, X-ray or CT scans, and health screening such as blood tests.
2. **Anaesthesia:** general anaesthesia, typically administered by an anaesthesiologist.
3. **Surgery:** bone cutting and reshaping through intraoral (and sometimes small external) incisions, with fixation as needed.
4. **Recovery:** hospital or clinic observation, often overnight, with compression garments and sometimes drains.

Surgery typically takes about 2–4 hours or longer depending on the combination.

## Recovery & downtime

Swelling is considerable and usually peaks in the first 3–5 days. Many people need 2–3 weeks before feeling comfortable in public, and most swelling resolves over 3–6 months, with final contours at 6–12 months. A soft or liquid diet is commonly required for several weeks, along with careful oral hygiene. Compression garments are often worn for a period. Strenuous exercise, alcohol and smoking are typically avoided for at least 4–6 weeks.

## Common side effects

- Major swelling, bruising and temporary difficulty opening the mouth
- Numbness of the lips, chin or cheeks, usually temporary but occasionally long-lasting
- Asymmetry or contour irregularities
- Sagging of soft tissue, especially after cheekbone reduction
- Rarely, infection, bone non-union, fracture or nerve injury
- Rare but serious risks such as significant bleeding, airway problems or anaesthesia complications

Discuss anaesthesia, safety monitoring and rare serious complications thoroughly with your surgeon.

## Before your visit

- Complete pre-operative health checks; disclose all medical conditions and medications.
- Stop smoking well in advance and review blood thinners as advised.
- Plan a stay of about 2–3 weeks in Korea and arrange a companion.
- Discuss flight timing with your surgeon; long-haul flights soon after major surgery may carry added risks such as blood clots.
- Consider travel insurance limitations; most do not cover cosmetic complications.

## Questions to ask your doctor

- Which bones need surgery for my goals, and which can be avoided?
- Is a dedicated anaesthesiologist present throughout?
- How do you protect nerves, and what is the risk of permanent numbness?
- What is the plan if bleeding or airway issues occur?
- How long should I stay before flying, and who follows me up after I return home?
