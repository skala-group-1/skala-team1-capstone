## Overview

Fat grafting (fat transfer) moves a patient's own fat from one area of the body, such as the thighs or abdomen, to areas that need volume. In the face it is commonly used for the forehead, temples, under-eye hollows, cheeks, nasolabial folds and chin. Because it uses your own tissue, results that survive can be long-lasting, but the amount of fat that "takes" is unpredictable.

**Also known as:** fat transfer, autologous fat injection, lipofilling, 지방이식, 얼굴 지방이식, 自体脂肪填充, 脂肪移植, 脂肪注入.

## How it works

Fat is harvested with a thin cannula using gentle suction, then processed (for example, by centrifugation or filtering) to separate usable fat cells from fluid and oil. The purified fat is injected in small amounts in multiple layers so it can develop a blood supply. Some of the transferred fat — often estimated at roughly 30–60%, though this varies widely — is reabsorbed over the first few months. A second session is sometimes needed. Some clinics offer enriched or "micro"/"nano" fat techniques for fine areas.

## Procedure steps

1. **Consultation:** assessment of facial volume loss and donor areas.
2. **Anaesthesia:** typically sedation or general anaesthesia; small cases may use local anaesthesia.
3. **Harvest:** fat is removed from the donor area through small incisions.
4. **Processing and injection:** fat is purified and injected into the face with fine cannulas.
5. **Recovery:** donor area compression is applied, and the patient rests before discharge.

The procedure typically takes about 1–2 hours.

## Recovery & downtime

Facial swelling is usually significant for 1–2 weeks and may make the face look overfilled at first. Bruising can last 1–2 weeks. The donor area may feel sore and bruised, and a compression garment may be recommended. The result usually stabilises after about 3–6 months once reabsorption has settled. Clinics commonly advise avoiding pressure or massage on grafted areas, sleeping elevated, and avoiding alcohol, smoking, saunas and strenuous exercise for several weeks.

## Common side effects

- Swelling, bruising and tenderness at face and donor sites
- Uneven absorption, asymmetry or lumps
- Overcorrection or undercorrection, sometimes needing further sessions
- Rarely, infection, cysts or oil cysts
- Rarely but seriously, fat entering a blood vessel (fat embolism), which can cause tissue damage or, very rarely, vision loss or stroke-like events

Discuss anaesthesia and rare serious complications with your surgeon.

## Before your visit

- Make sure you have enough donor fat; very lean patients may have limited supply.
- Stop smoking and review blood thinners and supplements as advised.
- Plan at least 7–10 days in Korea for early follow-up.
- Discuss flight timing with your surgeon.
- Avoid major weight changes after grafting, as grafted fat behaves like fat in the donor area.

## Questions to ask your doctor

- How much fat survival should I realistically expect?
- Where will fat be taken from, and will there be visible marks?
- Which anaesthesia will be used?
- How do you reduce the risk of fat entering blood vessels?
- Is a second session included or discounted if absorption is high?
