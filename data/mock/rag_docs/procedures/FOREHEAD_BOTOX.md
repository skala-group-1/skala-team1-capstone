## Overview

Forehead Botox is an injection of botulinum toxin into the muscles of the upper face to soften dynamic wrinkles — lines that appear when you raise your eyebrows or frown. It most often targets horizontal forehead lines and is frequently combined with treatment of the frown lines between the brows (glabella) or crow's feet. It is a quick, non-surgical treatment commonly offered as a "lunchtime" procedure.

**Also known as:** forehead wrinkle Botox, anti-wrinkle injection, 이마 보톡스, 주름 보톡스, 抬头纹肉毒, 额头除皱针, 額ボトックス.

## How it works

Botulinum toxin temporarily reduces the activity of the frontalis muscle, the muscle that lifts the brows and creates horizontal forehead creases. With reduced movement, the skin folds less, so existing lines may look softer and new lines may form more slowly. Results typically begin within 3–7 days, peak at around 2 weeks and last roughly 3–4 months. Duration varies by individual; your doctor will advise. Deep, static lines present at rest may only partially improve and may need other treatments.

## Procedure steps

1. **Consultation:** the doctor asks you to raise your brows and frown to map muscle activity and brow position.
2. **Preparation:** makeup is removed and the skin is cleansed; ice may be used for comfort.
3. **Injection:** a series of small, shallow injections is placed across the forehead using a fine needle.
4. **Check-out:** you can usually leave immediately.

The injection typically takes about 5–10 minutes.

## Recovery & downtime

There is usually little or no downtime. Small bumps at the injection points typically settle within 15–30 minutes. Clinics commonly advise you to stay upright for a few hours, avoid rubbing the area, and skip saunas, strenuous exercise and alcohol for about 24 hours. Many patients go back to normal activities the same day.

## Common side effects

- Redness, small bumps or pinpoint bruising at injection sites
- Mild headache in the first day or two
- A heavy or tight feeling in the forehead while you adjust
- Brow asymmetry or a "surprised" arch, which may be adjusted at follow-up
- Uncommonly, brow or eyelid drooping (ptosis) that typically resolves over weeks
- Allergic reactions are rare

Treatment is generally avoided during pregnancy, breastfeeding or with certain neuromuscular conditions.

## Before your visit

- Share your medication list, including blood thinners and supplements such as fish oil or vitamin E.
- Tell the doctor if your brows or eyelids already feel heavy, as this can affect the injection plan.
- Mention any recent Botox elsewhere, including dates and areas.
- If you are travelling, plan the visit at least a few days before important events so any bumps or bruises can settle.

## Questions to ask your doctor

- Are my lines dynamic or static, and how much improvement is realistic?
- Should the frown lines be treated at the same time to keep a balanced look?
- How do you avoid brow drooping in my case?
- When can I have a follow-up check, and is a touch-up included?
- How long should I wait before my next treatment?
