## Overview

Liposuction is a surgical procedure that removes localized fat deposits using thin tubes (cannulas) and suction. Common areas include the abdomen, flanks, thighs, arms, back and under the chin. It is intended for body contouring rather than weight loss, and works best for people near a stable weight with good skin elasticity.

**Also known as:** lipo, liposculpture, body contouring, 지방흡입, 복부 지방흡입, 허벅지 지방흡입, 吸脂, 抽脂, 脂肪吸引.

## How it works

The area is first infiltrated with a "tumescent" solution containing saline, local anaesthetic and a medication that constricts blood vessels, which reduces bleeding and discomfort. The surgeon then inserts cannulas through small incisions and removes fat. Energy-assisted methods (ultrasound, laser or power-assisted devices) may be used to loosen fat. Fat cells removed do not typically return, but remaining fat cells can still enlarge with weight gain.

## Procedure steps

1. **Consultation:** body assessment, skin quality, health screening and discussion of realistic goals.
2. **Anaesthesia:** sedation or general anaesthesia, depending on the area and volume.
3. **Marking and infiltration:** areas are marked and tumescent fluid injected.
4. **Fat removal:** fat is suctioned and the contour sculpted.
5. **Recovery:** incisions are closed or left to drain, and a compression garment is applied.

Surgery typically takes about 1–4 hours depending on areas treated.

## Recovery & downtime

Soreness, swelling, bruising and fluid drainage are common in the first days. Many people return to desk work after about 3–7 days for small areas, or 1–2 weeks for larger areas. Compression garments are often worn for 4–8 weeks. Swelling may take 3–6 months to fully settle before the final contour is seen. Gentle walking early on is commonly encouraged to support circulation; strenuous exercise is usually avoided for several weeks.

## Common side effects

- Swelling, bruising, soreness and temporary numbness
- Lumpy or uneven contours, or loose skin
- Fluid collections (seroma)
- Small scars at cannula sites, occasionally pigmented
- Rarely, infection or skin damage
- Rare but serious risks including blood clots (deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism), fat embolism, organ perforation, fluid imbalance or anaesthesia complications, especially with large-volume or multi-area procedures

Discuss anaesthesia, safe volume limits and rare serious complications with your surgeon.

## Before your visit

- Disclose medical history, clotting disorders and all medications.
- Stop smoking and review blood thinners and hormonal medications as advised.
- Plan at least 7–14 days in Korea and arrange a companion for the first days.
- Discuss flight timing with your surgeon; long flights after surgery may increase clot risk, so ask about prevention such as compression and movement.
- Prepare loose clothing and budget for compression garments.

## Questions to ask your doctor

- How much fat can be safely removed in one session for me?
- Which technique and anaesthesia will be used?
- Is an anaesthesiologist present, and how am I monitored?
- How do you reduce the risk of blood clots?
- When can I safely fly, and what if I develop a seroma after returning home?
