## Overview

Pico toning is a low-energy laser treatment using a picosecond laser, delivered over the whole face to improve uneven pigmentation, dullness, melasma-type pigmentation, freckles and overall skin tone. It is typically performed as a series of sessions. Picosecond lasers can also be used at higher settings for specific spots or tattoo removal, which is a different treatment with more downtime.

**Also known as:** picosecond laser toning, pico laser, skin toning laser, 피코토닝, 피코레이저, 皮秒激光, 皮秒美白, ピコトーニング.

## How it works

A picosecond laser emits extremely short pulses (trillionths of a second). These pulses break pigment into very small particles mainly through a pressure (photoacoustic) effect, with less heat than older nanosecond lasers. The body then gradually clears the fragmented pigment. Low-energy "toning" aims to lighten pigment gradually while limiting irritation. Improvement typically builds over a course of about 5–10 sessions spaced 1–4 weeks apart. Response varies by individual and pigment type; your doctor will advise.

## Procedure steps

1. **Consultation:** skin analysis to identify pigment type (for example, melasma versus freckles), since the plan differs.
2. **Preparation:** cleansing; numbing cream is often unnecessary but may be offered.
3. **Laser:** the handpiece is passed over the face; patients may feel light snapping or warmth.
4. **Soothing:** cooling or a calming mask, then sunscreen.

The laser itself typically takes about 10–20 minutes.

## Recovery & downtime

Downtime is usually minimal. Mild redness may last a few hours. Makeup is often allowed the same or next day. Strict sun protection is important during the course, as sun exposure may worsen pigmentation. Clinics commonly advise avoiding harsh exfoliants and retinoids for a few days.

## Common side effects

- Temporary redness, warmth or mild sensitivity
- Dryness
- Uncommonly, small scabs or tiny darkened spots
- Rarely, post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (darkening), particularly in darker skin types
- Rarely, hypopigmentation (light spots) with frequent or aggressive treatment

## Before your visit

- Tell the clinic about recent tanning, sunburn, isotretinoin or photosensitising medications.
- Mention a history of melasma or pigmentation that worsened after previous lasers.
- For short trips, ask whether one or two sessions will make a visible difference.
- Bring and use a broad-spectrum sunscreen and a hat.

## Questions to ask your doctor

- What type of pigmentation do I have, and is pico toning the best option?
- How many sessions are typical for my case, and how far apart?
- What should I use at home to maintain results?
- What is the risk of darkening for my skin type?
- Can this be combined with other treatments such as skin boosters?
