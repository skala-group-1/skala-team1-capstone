## Overview

Ptosis correction is surgery to raise an upper eyelid that sits too low over the eye, making the eyes look sleepy or tired and sometimes affecting vision. In cosmetic practice in Korea it is often called "eye opening surgery" or "nunmae correction" and may be combined with double eyelid surgery to make the eyes appear larger and more alert. True medical ptosis should be properly diagnosed, as causes vary.

**Also known as:** blepharoptosis repair, levator advancement, eye opening surgery, 눈매교정, 안검하수 교정, 提肌矫正, 上睑下垂矫正, 眼瞼下垂手術, 目力矯正.

## How it works

The upper eyelid is lifted mainly by the levator muscle and its tendon-like attachment (aponeurosis). In ptosis this mechanism is weak or stretched. Surgery typically shortens, tightens or reattaches it, either through an incision in the eyelid crease or, in mild cases, with a suture-based (non-incision) technique. In severe cases with very weak muscle, other techniques such as a frontalis sling may be considered. The surgeon balances lift with the ability to close the eye fully.

## Procedure steps

1. **Consultation:** measurement of eyelid height and levator function, check of eye dryness and vision, and screening for medical causes.
2. **Anaesthesia:** often local anaesthesia with sedation, so the patient can open the eyes during surgery to check height; general anaesthesia may be used in some cases.
3. **Correction:** the levator is adjusted, and skin or fat may be trimmed if combined with a double eyelid procedure.
4. **Height check and closure:** symmetry is assessed and the incision closed.

Surgery typically takes about 1–2 hours.

## Recovery & downtime

Swelling and bruising usually peak in the first few days and settle substantially over 1–2 weeks. Stitches are commonly removed around 5–7 days. The eyes may look stiff or uneven initially, with final results often taking 3–6 months. Temporary difficulty closing the eyes completely and dryness are common early on; lubricating eye drops may be prescribed. Clinics commonly advise cold compresses, sleeping elevated, and avoiding alcohol, smoking and strenuous exercise for several weeks.

## Common side effects

- Swelling, bruising and dryness or irritation
- Under- or over-correction, or asymmetry, which may need revision
- Incomplete eye closure (lagophthalmos), usually temporary
- Rarely, infection, bleeding or corneal exposure problems
- Rarely, changes to crease shape or eyelid contour

Discuss anaesthesia, rare serious complications and revision rates with your surgeon.

## Before your visit

- Bring any eye prescription and tell the doctor about dry eye, contact lens use, thyroid disease or previous eye surgery.
- Review blood thinners and smoking with your doctor ahead of time.
- Plan at least 7–10 days in Korea for stitch removal and a check-up.
- Discuss flight timing with your surgeon and bring prescribed eye drops for the flight.

## Questions to ask your doctor

- Do I have true ptosis, and what is causing it?
- Which technique do you recommend for my degree of ptosis?
- How do you check eyelid height during surgery?
- What is the chance I will need a revision?
- How will dry eye be managed, and when can I safely fly?
