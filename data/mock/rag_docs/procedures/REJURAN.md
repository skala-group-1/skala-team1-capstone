## Overview

Rejuran-type treatments use a polynucleotide-based injectable delivered in many small injections across the face. They are marketed as "skin healers" or "skin boosters" and are generally aimed at improving skin quality — texture, elasticity, fine lines, dullness, and sometimes enlarged pores or acne scars — rather than adding volume. Variants exist for the under-eye area and for scarring.

**Also known as:** skin healer, polynucleotide (PN) injection, salmon DNA injection, skin booster, 리쥬란, 리쥬란 힐러, 丽珠兰, 婴儿针, リジュラン.

## How it works

Polynucleotides are fragments of DNA-derived material that are thought to support the skin's natural repair processes and hydration. They are injected into the superficial layers of skin, where they may help improve the skin's environment over time. Effects are gradual: many people notice changes after 2–4 weeks. A course of around 2–4 sessions spaced about 2–4 weeks apart is often recommended, with maintenance every 6–12 months. Response varies by individual; your doctor will advise.

## Procedure steps

1. **Consultation:** skin assessment and agreement on treatment areas and number of sessions.
2. **Cleansing and numbing:** numbing cream is typically applied for 30–60 minutes because the treatment can be uncomfortable.
3. **Injection:** many tiny injections are given across the treatment area, by hand or with an injection device.
4. **Soothing:** a calming mask or cooling may be applied.

Injection typically takes about 15–30 minutes; the full visit often 1–1.5 hours with numbing.

## Recovery & downtime

Small raised bumps at each injection point are expected and usually flatten within about 1–3 days. Mild redness and swelling are common, and bruising may occur and last up to a week. Clinics commonly advise avoiding makeup for about 24 hours, and saunas, intense exercise, alcohol and active skincare (retinoids, exfoliants) for a few days. Strict sun protection is recommended.

## Common side effects

- Bumps, redness and swelling at injection sites
- Pain during treatment and tenderness afterwards
- Bruising
- Rarely, infection, prolonged lumps or allergic-type reactions
- People with fish allergies should mention this, as some products are derived from fish sources

## Before your visit

- Inform the clinic of allergies (especially to fish), skin conditions and current medications.
- Avoid blood thinners and supplements that increase bruising only if your own doctor agrees.
- Plan the visit early in your trip so bumps can settle before sightseeing photos or travel.
- Because a course is often recommended, ask whether a single session is worthwhile for a short stay.

## Questions to ask your doctor

- Which areas and which product variant do you recommend for my skin concerns?
- How many sessions will I need, and can they fit within my trip?
- Will you use hand injection or a device, and how does that affect comfort and bumps?
- Is the product sourced from fish, and does that matter for my allergies?
- Can this be combined with lasers or other treatments on the same day?
