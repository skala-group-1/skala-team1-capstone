## Overview

Rhinoplasty is surgery to change the shape of the nose. Common goals among patients in Korea include raising a low bridge, refining or lengthening the tip, reducing a hump, or narrowing the nostrils. It may also be combined with functional surgery to improve breathing. Rhinoplasty is considered one of the more technically demanding cosmetic surgeries, and revision rates are higher than for many other procedures.

**Also known as:** nose job, augmentation rhinoplasty, tip plasty, 코성형, 코수술, 융비술, 隆鼻手术, 鼻综合, 鼻整形, 隆鼻術.

## How it works

Depending on the plan, the surgeon may add height to the bridge with an implant (for example, silicone or expanded polytetrafluoroethylene) or the patient's own tissue, and reshape the tip using cartilage grafts taken from the septum, ear or rib. Humps may be reduced by shaving bone and cartilage, and nostrils narrowed by small excisions. Surgery may be "open" (a small incision across the columella) or "closed" (incisions inside the nose).

## Procedure steps

1. **Consultation:** facial analysis, nasal examination, sometimes CT imaging, and discussion of realistic goals.
2. **Anaesthesia:** usually general anaesthesia or deep sedation.
3. **Surgery:** cartilage harvesting if needed, reshaping, and implant placement if used.
4. **Closure and splinting:** incisions are closed and an external splint and sometimes internal packing are applied.

Surgery typically takes about 1.5–3 hours, longer for complex or revision cases.

## Recovery & downtime

Swelling and bruising around the nose and eyes are common for 1–2 weeks. The splint and stitches are commonly removed around 5–7 days. Many people feel ready for public settings after about 2 weeks, though the nose remains swollen; most swelling settles over 3–6 months, and the final shape, especially the tip, may take up to a year. Clinics commonly advise sleeping elevated, not blowing the nose, avoiding glasses resting on the bridge for several weeks, and avoiding alcohol, smoking and strenuous exercise for at least 3–4 weeks.

## Common side effects

- Swelling, bruising, nasal congestion and temporary numbness
- Asymmetry or a result different from expectations, which may need revision
- Rarely, infection, implant displacement, visibility or exposure of the implant
- Rarely, contracture, breathing difficulties or septal problems
- Rare serious risks related to anaesthesia, bleeding or skin compromise

Discuss anaesthesia, rare serious complications and revision policy with your surgeon.

## Before your visit

- Share previous nasal surgery or filler history and any breathing issues.
- Stop smoking and review blood thinners and supplements as advised.
- Plan at least 10–14 days in Korea, including splint removal and follow-up.
- Discuss flight timing with your surgeon; cabin pressure and swelling are considerations.
- Arrange a companion for the day of surgery.

## Questions to ask your doctor

- What exact changes do you recommend for my face, and why?
- Will you use an implant, my own cartilage, or both, and from where?
- Who provides anaesthesia, and what monitoring is in place?
- What is your revision policy and how often do your patients need revisions?
- When can I fly, and how can I be followed up after I return home?
